Everything for the circuits

The minimum parts to build each of the 26 "Circuits to build" projects and the two real-life builds (battery charger and night light), from gotronic.fr. Quantities are what the biggest project needs: you build one circuit at a time, then take it apart and reuse the parts for the next one.

How to use this list

  • Untick anything you already own (you probably have a soldering iron, wires, 9 V batteries, maybe an Arduino and a multimeter).
  • Change quantities if you want spares. Small parts cost a few cents, so getting 2–3 extra of each is a good idea for beginners.
  • Type the code into the Gotronic search box, or click the name to open the product page.
  • "Used in" refers to the project numbers on the Circuits to build page (A = battery charger, B = night light).

Prices include VAT (TTC), as shown on gotronic.fr on 18 Nov 2026, excluding delivery. They can change, and some items may go out of stock: Gotronic usually has close equivalents (same value, other brand). Resistors are sold in packs of 10, and the price shown is per pack.

Notes before you order

  • Li-ion safety: the 18650 cell sold here is unprotected, and the TP4056 charger page doesn't mention protection. So the list includes a BMS 1S protection board. Wire it as: cell → B+/B− of the BMS. Charger output and your load → P+/P−. Never short the cell, never charge it unattended the first times.
  • Relays: the RWH-SS relays have pins that don't fit a breadboard well. Solder 5 short wires (or header pins) to them first.
  • Mains: nothing on this list uses 230 V. The "lamp" is a 12 V bulb powered by a safe 12 V adapter.
  • Not on the list: 9 V batteries, a micro-USB cable for the charger, a multimeter (very useful, ~€15–25), a small screwdriver for the trimmers.
  • Inductor project (6): it uses the 5 V relay's coil as the inductor, so no extra part is needed.
