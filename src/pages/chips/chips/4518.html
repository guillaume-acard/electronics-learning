#CD4518 dual counter: counting in decimal

What it does: it contains two counters, A and B. Each one counts clock pulses from 0 to 9 and then starts again at 0, giving the count in BCD on 4 outputs (Q1 = 1, Q2 = 2, Q3 = 4, Q4 = 8): exactly what a CD4511 wants. Chain the two halves and you count from 00 to 99.

How it works inside: each counter is 4 flip-flops (1-bit memories) that count in binary, plus a little logic that jumps from 1001 (9) straight back to 0000 instead of going on to 10–15. A counter moves on each rising edge of CLK while EN is high, or on each falling edge of EN while CLK is low. That second way is the trick for chaining: the units' Q4 (the "8") drops when going 9 → 0, so wired to the tens' EN it adds one ten. RESET (high) sends it back to 0.

Water analogy: a row of buckets that each hold 9 cups. Every tick pours one cup into the first bucket. When it gets its 10th cup, it tips over, empties itself, and pours one cup into the next bucket: the units carry over into the tens.

Untick "chain" and counter B never moves: the carry is just one wire. Its cousins: the CD4017 (one output per count, above), and the CD4510 (counts up or down, can be preset, not sold at Gotronic). Used in project 19: counter 00–99.
