#ATmega328P: the brain of the Arduino

What it does: it's a complete little computer on one chip: a processor, 32 KB of memory for your program, 2 KB of working memory, and 23 input/output pins that your code can switch, read, time and measure. The Arduino Uno (and the ONE-C, with its cousin the ATmega328PB) is just this chip plus USB, a 16 MHz crystal and a 5 V regulator. It runs the dice (project 12) and the thermometer (project 20).

How it works inside: a CPU reads your program from Flash memory and executes ~16 million simple instructions per second, ticking with the crystal. It talks to the outside through ports (B, C, D): each pin is a tiny switch to 5 V or 0 V (digitalWrite), or an input (digitalRead). Some pins have extras: an ADC (analogRead, 6 pins A0–A5, 10 bits = 0–1023), timers for PWM (analogWrite on 6 pins), serial (TX/RX to the PC).

Water analogy: a control room with 23 valves and gauges, and an operator (the CPU) who follows a written procedure (your program) 16 million steps per second.

Potentiometer on A0

The demo runs a tiny program: value = analogRead(A0); analogWrite(9, value / 4); digitalWrite(13, millis() / 500 % 2);. Arduino pin names (D0–D13, A0–A5) are not the chip's pin numbers: D13 is chip pin 19, A0 is pin 23.
