#L293D H-bridge: a motor forwards and backwards

What it does: it drives two DC motors in both directions, from logic signals (buttons, an Arduino). Up to 600 mA per motor (1.2 A peaks), with a motor supply from 4.5 to 36 V on pin 8 and a separate logic supply on pin 16. The "D" means the flyback diodes are built in: no extra diodes for the motor's switch-off spikes.

How it works inside: the H-bridge. To reverse a DC motor, you reverse the current through it. An H-bridge is four switches arranged like the letter H, with the motor as the middle bar. Close top-left and bottom-right: current flows left → right. Close the other diagonal: right → left. The L293D has four "half-bridges" (1Y, 2Y, 3Y, 4Y): each output is switched either to + or to ground, copying its input. Two outputs make one H-bridge. Because each output is either + or −, never both, you can't make the deadly short circuit of closing both switches on one side.

Water analogy: a water wheel in a pipe between two tanks. Raise the left tank and lower the right: it turns one way. Swap them: it turns the other way. Same level on both sides: it stops. The L293D is the one lifting the tanks.

Good to know: the L293D uses bipolar transistors, which lose about 1.5–3 V in total, so from a 9 V battery the motor gets about 6–7 V, and the chip gets warm (the 4 middle ground pins are its heat sink). Modern MOSFET drivers (TB6612, DRV8833) lose much less. Used in project 24: motor forward / reverse. How the motor itself works: Sound & motion.
