#TP4056 Li-ion charger: filling a battery safely

What it does: it charges a single Li-ion / Li-po cell (like an 18650) from 5 V USB, the safe way: first with a constant current (up to 1 A), then with a constant voltage of exactly 4.2 V while the current fades, then it stops. Two pins drive a red "charging" and a green "done" LED. It's the chip on the charger module of real-life build A, a tiny 8-pin SOP soldered on the board.

How it works inside: a power transistor between VCC and BAT acts as an adjustable valve. A control loop watches the battery: below 4.2 V it regulates the current (set by the PROG resistor: I = 1200 ÷ R); at 4.2 V it switches to holding the voltage. When the current falls to a tenth of the setting, the cell is full: CHRG turns off, STDBY turns on. A thermal loop lowers the current if the chip gets too hot.

Water analogy: filling a bottle from a tap: full flow at first, then you slow down as you approach the brim, and stop exactly at the line. Over-filling a Li-ion cell (above ~4.25 V) is dangerous, hence the precise chip.

PROG resistor

The TP4056 does not protect against over-discharge or short circuits: that's the job of the separate protection chip (DW01A + dual MOSFET) on the BMS module, or on "protected" TP4056 boards (6 pins: IN+, IN−, B+, B−, OUT+, OUT−).
