#LM386 audio amplifier: drives a speaker

What it does: it takes a small audio signal and gives it enough power (up to ~0.5–1 W) to move a speaker cone. It's the heart of countless mini amps, including your Ruby amp.

How it works inside: an input stage, a gain stage, then a push-pull output: two transistors, one that pushes the speaker out (from +9 V) and one that pulls it in (to ground). The gain is 20× by default, and up to 200× if you connect a capacitor between pins 1 and 8. In the Ruby, the 1 kΩ gain knob sits there, so you choose anywhere in between.

Water analogy: two pumps facing a flexible membrane (the speaker cone). One pushes, the other pulls, in time with the music.

🎸 Guitar signal Gain knob (pins 1–8)

The output sits at half the supply (4.5 V) and swings up and down around it. That's why there's a big 220 µF capacitor before the speaker: it blocks the 4.5 V DC and passes the music, exactly the coupling trick from the capacitor page. Push the gain too far and the swing hits 0 V / 9 V and clips, which is the Ruby's overdrive.
