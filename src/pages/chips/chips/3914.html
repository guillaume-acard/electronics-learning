#LM3914 bar-graph driver: a voltage becomes a bar

What it does: it lights 1 to 10 LEDs depending on a voltage: a little voltage lights the first LED, the full-scale voltage lights all ten. It's a ready-made "level meter": battery gauges, VU-meters, thermometers (project 21 builds the same thing by hand with 3 × LM339, and lists the LM3914 as a variant), no microcontroller needed. It also regulates the LED current itself, so no resistor per LED.

How it works inside: a ladder of 10 equal resistors between RLO and RHI makes 10 evenly spaced voltages. 10 comparators (op-amps, see the LM358) each compare the input with one rung: if the input is higher, that comparator turns its LED on. A built-in 1.25 V reference lets you set the scale with a trimmer, and the current it supplies (× 10) sets the LED brightness. MODE chooses a bar (all LEDs up to the level) or a single moving dot.

Water analogy: a tall tank with 10 overflow holes at equal heights, each feeding a little light. The higher the water, the more holes spill.

Input (SIG)RHI (top of scale)

Cousins: the LM3915 (logarithmic steps of 3 dB, for audio) and LM3916 (VU-meter scale). Chain two LM3914s for 20 LEDs. Gotronic: LM3914N, code 21122.
