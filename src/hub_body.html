Start here: the water analogy

Every page uses the same idea: electricity behaves like water flowing in pipes. Keep this table in mind and the rest gets much easier.

Water
Electricity
Pump
→
Battery / power supply
Pressure
→
Voltage, in volts (V)
Flow of water
→
Current, in amps (A)
Narrow pipe
→
Resistance, in ohms (Ω)
River (one direction, steady)
→
DC: Direct Current
Waves sloshing back and forth
→
AC: Alternating Current

And the one formula that ties them together: V = I × R, which in water terms is pressure = flow × narrowness.

Components

Resistor

LED

A diode that glows. Always needs a resistor. Includes a resistor calculator, PWM dimming and RGB colour mixing.

Transistor

A small current controls a big one. A switch for motors, an amplifier for guitars, and the logic behind computers.

Inductor

A coil that resists changes in current. Boost converters, filters, transformers, the switch-off spike.

A switch moved by an electromagnet. Isolated control of lamps, motors and mains, plus self-latching memory.
