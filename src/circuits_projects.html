1LED brightness with different resistors

Resistor See how the resistor sets the current, and so the brightness. 📖 lesson

🧰 You need

  • 9 V battery + clip
  • 1 red LED (5 mm)
  • Resistors: 220 Ω, 470 Ω, 1 kΩ, 10 kΩ
  • Breadboard + jumper wires

🔧 Build

  1. Battery + → resistor → LED long leg (anode).
  2. LED short leg → battery −.

🧪 Variants to try

Swap 220 Ω → 470 Ω → 1 kΩ → 10 kΩThe LED gets dimmer as R grows (I = 7 V ÷ R).
Put two 1 kΩ in seriesSame as 2 kΩ: dimmer.
Put two 1 kΩ side by side (parallel)Same as 500 Ω: brighter.
Turn the LED roundNothing: it's a one-way valve.

2LED that fades out slowly

Capacitor Watch a capacitor act as an energy reserve. 📖 lesson

🧰 You need

  • 9 V battery
  • Push button
  • Electrolytic capacitors: 100 µF, 470 µF, 1000 µF (16 V or more)
  • 470 Ω resistor, red LED

🔧 Build

  1. Battery + → button → a node.
  2. From the node: capacitor + to the node, capacitor − (stripe) to battery −.
  3. From the node: 470 Ω → LED → battery −.

🧪 Variants to try

100 µF vs 1000 µFA bigger capacitor gives a longer fade (more stored charge).
220 Ω instead of 470 ΩBrighter, but a shorter fade (it empties faster).
Remove the capacitorThe LED snaps off instantly.
Measure the capacitor with a multimeter while fadingYou can watch the voltage drop.

3Polarity tester (which way is +?)

Diode Two LEDs back to back show which way the current flows. 📖 lesson

🧰 You need

  • 9 V battery
  • 1 red + 1 green LED
  • 1 kΩ resistor
  • 2 test wires

🔧 Build

  1. Put the LEDs in parallel but opposite ways (red long leg with green short leg).
  2. Add the 1 kΩ in series with the pair.
  3. Touch the two wires to a battery.

🧪 Variants to try

Swap the battery wiresThe other colour lights: each LED only conducts one way.
Add a 1N4007 diode in seriesOnly one colour can ever light: the diode blocks the other direction.
Measure across a lit LEDAbout 2 V (red) or 2–3 V (green): the forward voltage.

4Mini traffic light

LED Three colours, each with its own resistor (and why). 📖 lesson

🧰 You need

  • 9 V battery (or 5 V USB)
  • Red, yellow, green LEDs
  • 3 × 470 Ω resistors
  • 3 push buttons

🔧 Build

  1. Each LED gets its own 470 Ω resistor and its own button to +.
  2. All LED short legs go to −.

🧪 Variants to try

Press all 3 at onceAll three light equally.
Try ONE shared resistor for all 3Uneven brightness: one LED hogs the current.
Swap red for blueA blue LED needs ~3 V, so it's a bit dimmer with the same resistor.

5Touch switch (skin turns on an LED)

Transistor Two transistors amplify the tiny current through your finger ~10,000×. 📖 lesson

🧰 You need

  • 9 V battery
  • 2 × BC547 (NPN)
  • 1 kΩ + 470 Ω resistors
  • LED, 2 bare wires as touch pads

🔧 Build

  1. Transistor 1 emitter → transistor 2 base (a "Darlington pair").
  2. Both collectors → LED → 470 Ω → +9 V.
  3. Transistor 2 emitter → −.
  4. Pad A → +9 V via 1 kΩ, pad B → transistor 1 base.

🧪 Variants to try

Touch both pads with one fingerThe LED lights: your skin lets a tiny current flow.
Use only one transistorDim or nothing: not enough gain.
Wet finger vs dry fingerWet skin conducts better, so it's brighter.

6The coil kick-back (inductive spike)

Inductor Make a coil's "water hammer" visible with an LED. 📖 lesson

🧰 You need

  • 9 V battery
  • A relay (use its coil) or a small 10–100 mH inductor
  • Push button
  • 1 LED (no resistor needed here)
  • 1N4007 diode (for the variant)

🔧 Build

  1. Battery → button → coil → battery −.
  2. Put the LED across the coil backwards (long leg towards the battery −).

🧪 Variants to try

Press, then release the buttonThe LED flashes on release: the spike reverses direction and lights it.
Replace the LED with a 1N4007 diode (same orientation)No flash: the diode absorbs the spike safely (that's a flyback diode).
Use a coil with more turnsA bigger kick.

7Relay clicker with two lamps

Relay A small button controls a separate circuit through a relay. 📖 lesson

🧰 You need

  • 5 V relay (or a relay module)
  • BC547 + 1 kΩ + 1N4007
  • Push button
  • 2 LEDs + 2 × 470 Ω (as "lamps") + separate 9 V battery

🔧 Build

  1. Button → 1 kΩ → BC547 base. Relay coil between +5 V and the collector, with the diode across the coil (stripe to +).
  2. On the switch side: COM → 9 V +. NC → red LED + resistor. NO → green LED + resistor.

🧪 Variants to try

Press the buttonClick: red goes off and green goes on.
Remove the flyback diode (briefly!)It works, but the transistor gets spikes: it may die over time.
Use a 9 V battery for the coil tooA 5 V relay on 9 V gets hot: match the coil voltage.

8555 blinker (and buzzer)

Microchips The classic first chip project. 📖 lesson

🧰 You need

  • NE555, 9 V battery
  • R1 1 kΩ, R2 10–100 kΩ
  • C 10 µF
  • LED + 470 Ω
  • (variant) 10 nF capacitor + small speaker or piezo

🔧 Build

  1. Pins: 8 & 4 → +9 V, 1 → −.
  2. R1 between 8 and 7, R2 between 7 and 6, link 6–2.
  3. C from 2/6 to −. Pin 3 → 470 Ω → LED → −.

🧪 Variants to try

Change R2 (10 k → 100 k)Slower blinking.
C 10 µF → 100 µF10× slower.
C 10 nF + speaker on pin 3A tone you can hear!
A pot as R2An adjustable speed (or pitch).

9LED chaser: 555 + CD4017

Microchips Make a light that runs along 10 LEDs. 📖 lesson

🧰 You need

  • The 555 blinker above
  • CD4017
  • 10 LEDs + 10 × 470 Ω (or one shared, since only one is on at a time)

🔧 Build

  1. 555 pin 3 → 4017 pin 14 (clock).
  2. 4017: 16 → +, 8 → −, 13 → −, 15 → − (reset).
  3. Each output Q0–Q9 → LED → −.

🧪 Variants to try

Change the 555 speedThe chaser speeds up or slows down.
Wire Q5 to pin 15 (reset)It now counts 0–4 only.
Arrange the LEDs in a circleA spinning-wheel effect.

10Mini speaker amp (LM386)

Microchips The core of your Ruby amp: plug in a phone or guitar. 📖 lesson

🧰 You need

  • LM386, 9 V battery
  • 10 kΩ pot (volume)
  • 220 µF + 100 nF + 10 µF capacitors
  • 8 Ω speaker, audio jack

🔧 Build

  1. Pins 4 → −, 6 → +9 V. Input via the volume pot to pin 3, pin 2 → −.
  2. Pin 5 → 220 µF (+ side) → speaker → −.
  3. 100 nF + 10 Ω from pin 5 to − (Zobel). 100 µF across the battery.

🧪 Variants to try

Add 10 µF between pins 1 and 8Gain 20 → 200: much louder, and it distorts sooner.
Remove the 220 µFThe speaker gets DC: a thump, hum, wasted power.
Turn up the volume a lotClipping = overdrive.
